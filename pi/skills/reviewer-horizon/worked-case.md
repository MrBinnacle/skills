# Worked case: a curated brief manufactured a collision, 2026-09-06

Read this for the shape of the failure. The procedure in `SKILL.md` does not depend on it.

Observed 2026-09-06, on a published skill collection.

Two authoring rules had been added to a repository's `AGENTS.md`. Fourteen cards were about to be migrated against them. A reviewer was asked how to run the migration and was sent **the two new rules and one size rule** — not the conventions section they live in.

It returned a strong answer with a card template ending in a `## References` section. That section is forbidden by a rule five bullets away from the ones supplied: *"Cross-references — inline at moment-of-need. No trailing 'Related' / 'See also' section."*

The first write-up said the tree overrides the reviewer. Challenged on it, two things came out. The exemplar could not adjudicate: the card both rules were drawn from contains zero markdown links, so it says nothing about cross-referencing. And the corpus had obeyed the prohibition while ignoring the positive half — 21 of 47 reader-facing auxiliaries were named nowhere in their own card, including one card's format specification that the card depends on.

So the house rule was defective too, in a way nobody had looked for, because the collision had been filed as the reviewer's error.

The brief was re-sent with the full conventions section and an explicit instruction that the rules were evidence about one maintainer's decisions rather than constraints. The second answer repaired five rules. The six-item list above is that reviewer's answer to "what should I have sent."
